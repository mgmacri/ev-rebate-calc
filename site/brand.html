EVDeals.ca

Brand and design system

EVDeals.ca is a straight-talking Canadian tool with a soft spot for the corny stuff. It should feel like a good hardware store run by a guy in a toque: warm, sturdy, honest about prices, and not shy about where it is from. The beaver is our spirit animal. He is allowed to be a bit of a ham.

Idea

Built here. Priced straight. Everything on the site exists to answer one question fast: what will this EV really cost me after the federal rebate, right now, in Canada.

The mascot reference is a beaver with a flag. We keep the sentiment (industrious, friendly, national) and drop the cartoon rendering. The mark is flat, two-tone, and reads at 16 pixels.

Three words to check any screen against: warm, sturdy, hokey. If it feels slick, cold, or salesy, it is wrong. If it makes you groan a little and smile anyway, it is right.

Mark

A flat beaver head on a red rounded square. Two teeth, no outlines, no gradients. The maple leaf stays on the flag and in the favicon only. A toque is fine for seasonal use. No sunglasses, no hockey stick, no animation.

Rules

  • Red is loud on purpose, so it is rationed: the flag bands top and bottom, the ".ca", the active tab, one primary button per screen, and the leaf.
  • Brown does the structural work. Umber for rules and headings, pelt for secondary emphasis, tan for stripes, badges and callouts.
  • Green means eligible and nothing else. Red text means over cap or not eligible. Never use red for decoration inside data.
  • No dark mode. The brand lives in daylight on paper.

Type

Barlow 400 for body. Seventeen pixels base, because our readers are not twenty-two. Lines under sixty-five characters.
Barlow 400 small, muted. Table headers, footnotes, sources.

One family, two widths. The condensed cut carries the personality: it reads like highway signage and hockey numbers. The regular cut does the reading.

  • Headlines are sentences with a full stop, in sentence case. No all caps labels anywhere.
  • Numbers use tabular figures and right-align in tables.
  • Do not colour a single word in a headline for emphasis.

Voice

Write like a neighbour who did the homework and is a little corny about it. Short sentences. Kilometres, not miles. Name the town when it helps. "The feds" or "Ottawa" when talking to buyers, "Transport Canada" when citing the source. Hokey is the house style: lean into the Canadiana, just keep the numbers dead straight.

Say

  • Sticker price minus the rebate, on every EV Ottawa will chip in for.
  • Works from Tofino to Torbay.
  • Made in Canada, eh.
  • Built for buyers, not for dealers. Sorry, dealers.
  • Grab a double-double and have a look.
  • Beauty. That one qualifies.
  • Loonies, toonies, toques, the feds, a rig, a beauty, a bit of a ham.

Do not say

  • "Elbows up" or anything built on it. Banned in all copy, alt text and social, no exceptions.
  • "Unlock", "supercharge", "game-changing", "seamless". Hokey is not the same as salesy.
  • Jokes at anyone's expense, or at any province's expense.
  • More than one exclamation mark per screen.
  • Corny copy anywhere a number lives. Prices, caps and dates are always plain.
  • Any automaker's name or logo as if we were affiliated.

Layout

  • Flag bands: a 10px red band at the very top and bottom of every page. This is the one piece of chrome that never changes.
  • Content width 1180px for data pages, 920px for reading. Left aligned. 16px side gutters on phones.
  • Cards are white on paper with a hairline and a soft umber shadow. Radius 12px on cards, 8px on controls, 4px on callouts.
  • Hairlines are tan-brown, never grey.
